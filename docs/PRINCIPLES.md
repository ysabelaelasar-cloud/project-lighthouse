# 🏮 Lighthouse Principles

> "Know your limits before your body has to teach them to you."

These principles guide every design decision, feature request, algorithm, and recommendation within Project Lighthouse.

If a future feature conflicts with these principles, the principles take precedence.

---

# 1. The User Is the Expert

Lighthouse never assumes it knows the user better than the user knows themselves.

The purpose of Lighthouse is to augment judgment, not replace it.

Recommendations should support informed decisions rather than dictate behavior.

---

# 2. Explain Everything

Every recommendation should answer:

"Why?"

Users should always be able to inspect the factors contributing to a recommendation.

Black-box scores without explanation should be avoided.

---

# 3. Respect Uncertainty

Health is inherently uncertain.

When confidence is low, Lighthouse should say so.

Rather than pretending certainty exists, Lighthouse communicates probabilities, confidence levels, and unknowns honestly.

---

# 4. Personal Data Over Population Data

Whenever sufficient personal history exists, recommendations should prioritize the user's own data over generalized averages.

The user's lived experience is the primary source of truth.

---

# 5. Reduce Cognitive Load

Many users will interact with Lighthouse while fatigued, symptomatic, or cognitively impaired.

Every screen should be usable under those conditions.

Complexity belongs behind the interface—not on it.

---

# 6. Recommendations Must Be Actionable

Information alone is rarely enough.

Every recommendation should help answer:

"What should I do next?"

---

# 7. Support, Never Judge

Lighthouse does not reward or punish.

It does not celebrate "good" days or criticize "bad" days.

Instead, it helps users understand patterns without assigning blame.

---

# 8. Preserve Autonomy

The user always makes the final decision.

Lighthouse offers guidance, not commands.

Language matters.

Prefer:

"Based on your history..."

over

"You shouldn't..."

---

# 9. Learn Continuously

Every day of new data improves Lighthouse's understanding.

Recommendations should evolve as the user's condition evolves.

---

# 10. Privacy Is Foundational

Health data belongs to the user.

Sharing should always be explicit.

Users should understand what data is collected, why it is used, and how it contributes to recommendations.

---

# 11. Small Improvements Matter

Success is not measured only by preventing crashes.

Helping a user safely attend a family gathering, complete an errand, or recover more confidently is meaningful progress.

---

# 12. Transparency Builds Trust

Users should be able to trace every recommendation back to its contributing evidence.

Trust is earned through clarity, not complexity.

---

# 13. Build With Compassion

Every interaction should acknowledge that chronic illness can be frustrating, unpredictable, and isolating.

The application should make difficult days feel more manageable—not more overwhelming.

---

# Our Goal

Project Lighthouse exists to help people navigate uncertainty with greater confidence.

It cannot remove the storm.

It can help illuminate a safer path through it.

---

## Our North Star

Project Lighthouse exists to help people make better decisions—not perfect decisions.

Success is not measured by eliminating symptoms or preventing every crash.

Success is measured by helping users spend their limited energy on the people, experiences, and goals that matter most to them, while understanding the tradeoffs involved.
